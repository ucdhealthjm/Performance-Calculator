For perspective: sleep loss and alcohol

In a study of 34 pediatric residents, performance after heavy night call was comparable to performance during a light-call rotation at a blood alcohol concentration of 0.04–0.05%, on tests of attention, vigilance, and simulated driving. Arnedt et al., JAMA (2005).

In a study of 39 adults, 17–19 hours without sleep produced performance equivalent to or worse than a blood alcohol concentration of 0.05%; after longer periods awake, some tests reached levels equivalent to 0.1%. Williamson & Feyer, Occup Environ Med (2000).

These comparisons come from prior research, not from this simulation.

About the calculations

Results are model means at the selected sleep duration. The composite averages clinical accuracy, diagnostic accuracy, reaction time, and attentional vigilance on a 0–100 scale; comparisons use an eight-hour rested baseline. Ranges reflect the model’s parameter and observation variability. Accuracy range endpoints are bounded to 0–100% for display. Lapse ranges describe the underlying rate; error and burnout ranges describe probabilities.

The article groups sleep into hourly categories, while this calculator evaluates the exact value entered. The optional sensitivity setting increases the linear sleep-deficit term for error and the linear and quadratic sleep-deficit terms for lapses by 50%.

Related research

Duty hours and patient outcomes

FIRST met its noninferiority criterion for death or serious surgical complications (9.1% flexible vs 9.0% standard). iCOMPARE met its primary mortality criterion; unadjusted 30-day readmissions and prolonged stays did not meet their secondary noninferiority criteria.

FIRST 2016 · iCOMPARE 2019

Education and well-being

iCOMPARE found similar time in patient care and education. Interns in flexible programs reported greater dissatisfaction with well-being (OR 2.47) and personal life (OR 6.11). FIRST found mixed effects across personal-life and training experiences.

iCOMPARE education 2018 · FIRST 2016

Sleep and workload

ROSTERS residents slept 52.9 vs 49.1 hours per week on schedules without vs with extended shifts. Serious-error rates were higher overall without extended shifts, with substantial site variation and greater workload. The increase was no longer evident after adjustment for patients per resident.

ROSTERS sleep 2019 · Patient safety 2020

Recovery through napping

The 2026 R-NAP trial enrolled 35 anesthesia residents. In the primary analysis of 27 residents, a 30-minute nap opportunity after a 24-hour shift improved simulated clinical performance by 14.8 points on a 200-point scale (95% CI 2.8–26.9). Technical skills alone did not differ significantly.

Schmidt et al., Anesthesiology 2026
